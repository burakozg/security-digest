Target reader: Security Consultant (strategic, advisory focus). Prioritise business impact, trends, and strategic implications. De-prioritise operational/tactical content.

Summarise in 1-2 sentences. Focus on why it matters to a consultant — not technical CVE details or granular threat actor specifics.

Assign a domain and a category. These are two independent judgements: the domain is
about the SUBJECT, the category is about the KIND of writing. Every combination is
possible.

Domain — exactly one of: security | ai_security | ai_ml
- security: conventional information security — incidents, threats, vulnerabilities,
  defence, governance, the security industry. The default when AI is merely mentioned
  in passing rather than being what the item is actually about.
- ai_security: where AI and security genuinely meet — attacks on AI systems (prompt
  injection, model extraction, data poisoning), AI used offensively by attackers, AI
  used defensively by security teams, LLM/agent safety, AI governance where the point
  is risk. A security story is NOT ai_security merely because a vendor mentions AI.
- ai_ml: AI and machine learning with no security angle — model and product launches,
  research results, capabilities, adoption, funding, policy and regulation.

Category — exactly one of: news | thought_leadership | methods | other | exclude
- news: something that happened. Incidents, breaches, active exploitation, launches,
  announcements, funding, regulatory action, company and industry developments.
- thought_leadership: someone's argument. Analysis, opinion, commentary, predictions,
  strategy, market trends, lessons drawn from events.
- methods: something a reader could adopt or must now defend against. New techniques,
  attack methods, defensive approaches, frameworks, methodologies, architectures,
  tooling approaches, research introducing a new way of doing something.
- other: none of the above, but still worth the reader's time. Use this rather than
  forcing an awkward fit — it is the residual, not a judgement about importance.
- exclude: not worth delivering at all. Non-critical CVEs, patch notes, granular threat
  actor technical updates, operational how-to, very specific vendor/tool updates.

If an item could plausibly take two categories, prefer the one describing what the
reader gains: a piece announcing a new attack technique is methods, not news.
